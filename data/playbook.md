**Caveat: this is about 38 rows, roughly 21 unique clips, most cross-posted to both platforms. Treat every rule as a weak signal, not a proven law.**

1. **Pick xQc police/RP clips with one clear absurd premise.** The top YouTube results were all xQc: freestyle 15.7k, "cops call about the car you used in a robbery" 10.7k, "scammed out of 20k" 4.5k, "flag your own car as stolen" 3.5k, "18 cops / gun hurts wrist" 2.5k.
2. **Pick the "dumb confident line digging a hole" kind of humor.** The best clip by far was "He freestyled himself into a corner and there's no way out 💀" (23.8s). It had 585 likes on 15.7k views, about 3.7% likes per view, which is the best like rate on YouTube.
3. **Write hooks that state the whole joke in one short sentence.** Use a concrete character or situation plus a twist, e.g. "He told a customer he got scammed out of 20k 💀" or "POV: cops call about the car you used in a robbery 💀". "POV:" hooks are a safe default. Almost all of them reached the ~1.3–1.5k YouTube baseline or better.
4. **Stop picking clips whose hook needs context or is a long multi-clause run-on.** These flopped on both platforms (about 10 views each):
   - "When you say you smacked three guys and he heard you screamed"
   - "POV: you jump off a roof then gaslight a stranger about the crack" (50s)
   - "POV: you trash talk the cop on the radio and he's standing right behind you"
   - "She got stuck in a vending machine and introduced herself"
5. **Be cautious with vanillamace and the Cinna "reaction"-only clips.** The only vanillamace clip got 12 YouTube views. Cinna's two weakest clips (the rolling-R's rizz clip, 232 YouTube views, and the roof clip, 12 views) were both reaction/funny_line with a weak premise. Cinna's chaos/rp_drama clips with a clear premise did fine (bathroom 1.0k TikTok / 1.3k YouTube, downed-crew 1.2k YouTube). Pick Cinna only for a strong, easily stated scenario.
6. **Keep the webcam on when possible.** 20 of 21 clips had it on, and the one without (the Cinna rizz clip) was among the worst. That is only one example, so don't rule on it, but don't pick no-webcam clips until it's tested.
7. **Clip length: aim for roughly 22–50s, but don't over-optimize.** Every clip under 30s that was kept ran ~1.3–1.5k views on YouTube, plus the 15.7k winner. The 33–49s winners had higher variance. Length did not clearly separate winners from losers, so choose by joke strength.
8. **YouTube Shorts is the main driver.** It got about 1.2–15k views on nearly every clip with a decent premise. TikTok views were far lower (0–1k) on the same clips. Keep posting to both, but judge clips by YouTube.
9. **Investigate TikTok before drawing conclusions.** Five xQc police/RP clips got 0 views or 1 view on TikTok while the same clips got 3.5k–10.7k on YouTube. That looks like a distribution, upload or moderation problem, not a bad clip. Check these for strikes, muted audio or copyright flags. Don't conclude that police content fails on TikTok.
10. **Don't trust the internal "score" field.** Nearly everything is a 7. The two 8-scored clips with the worst hooks got 1 and 12 views, and the 8-scored bathroom clip was average. Don't use it to choose clips.
11. **Posting hour has no clear winner.** Both big and tiny clips posted at the same hours (19:00 had 10.7k and 10 views; 09:00–11:00 had both the 15.7k clip and solid baseline clips). The clip mattered far more than the time. Keep posting in the 09:00–13:00 window while testing a few evening slots, one clip per slot.
12. **Likes and shares.** Shares are about 0 everywhere and carry no signal. YouTube likes and shares are mostly missing. On TikTok, likes per view was highest for the "bathroom drive" (5.7%), "wife on speaker" (4.6%) and "scammed out of 20k" (4.4%) clips. Those are relatable-awkward, everyday-situation jokes.
