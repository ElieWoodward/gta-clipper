**Caveat:** about 14 unique clips, each posted to both platforms. Most are xQc or Cinna, with 1 vanillamace clip. Posting hour, length and webcam show no reliable pattern, so the rules below are leanings, not proof.

1. **Pick more xQc police and authority-figure bits.** Cops, interviews and customers did best. YouTube views: "cops call about the car you used in a robbery" 11.4k, "ask the cops to flag your own car as stolen" 3.5k, "18 cops… gun hurts your wrist" 2.5k, "cop makes you put your wife on speaker" 1.7k.
2. **Pick clips where one absurd, easy-to-picture situation is set up and then pays off.** Examples: the interview with the war crimes line (2.4k), telling a customer he got scammed out of 20k (4.5k), and the boss's speech buffering (1.4k from a 23s clip). Rules 1 and 2 are the strongest signals in the data.
3. **Use funny_line and rp_drama as the core tags.** Add police to them when you can. Chaos or reaction alone is a weaker pick. Cinna's downed-crew clip, tagged reaction/chaos, did only 1.25k on YouTube and 360 on TikTok.
4. **Stop picking clips that need backstory or a long setup.** The vending-machine clip (438 on TikTok, 9 on YouTube) and "jump off a roof then gaslight a stranger" (0 and 9) both flopped. The same goes for "smacked three guys and he heard you screamed" (0 and 7) and "trash talk the cop… standing right behind you" (0 and 7). The last one had the right topic, but its hook is 14 words long and gives away the payoff.
5. **Hook style: "POV:" plus one concrete, short scenario, ending in 💀 or 😭.** It was used on most of the hits. Good examples: "POV: cops call about the car you used in a robbery 💀" and "POV: you beg a stranger to drive you to the bathroom 😭💀". Keep hooks to about 12 words or fewer. Name the specific absurd detail (20k, 18 cops, wife on speaker).
6. **Third-person "He told a customer…" hooks also worked.** "He told a customer he got scammed out of 20k 💀" got 4.5k on YouTube. Avoid vague hooks like "When you say you…" and hooks that give away the payoff.
7. **Treat TikTok as unreliable right now.** 5 of 14 clips got 0–1 views there, including clips that did 3k–11k on YouTube. Don't judge a clip's quality by its TikTok result alone. Check whether the zero-view TikToks were removed or flagged (the police/robbery hooks are possible suspects). Until you know, post to YouTube first.
8. **Prioritise YouTube Shorts for xQc police and RP clips.** YouTube beat TikTok on 11 of 14 pairs. TikTok did best with relatable, human-scale bits. Cinna's bathroom clip got 1,036 views and 59 likes, about 5.7% likes per view. The wife-on-speaker clip got 1,040 views and 48 likes. Use those two as the template for TikTok.
9. **Keep length around 30–50s.** The hits ran 23–49s, but flops fell in the same range, so length is not a deciding factor. Don't spend effort cutting for length. Spend it on picking a clip with a clear payoff in the first few seconds.
10. **Webcam: keep it on, but this is not a proven factor.** Nearly every clip had the webcam on. The only webcam-off clip ("rolling his R's was rizz") was low on both platforms (108 and 231 views), but one clip proves nothing. If you test, test it deliberately.
11. **Don't commit to posting times yet.** Hits and flops are spread across all hours (11:00, 16:00 and 19:00 appear on both sides). Run a deliberate test next week with the same type of clip at different hours.
12. **Shares were near zero (TikTok data only) and YouTube has no share data.** Judge humor by likes per view, and ignore shares. The best likes-per-view clips were the relatable human situations (bathroom, wife on speaker) on TikTok. The biggest view counts came from cop and authority situations on YouTube. Use both kinds.
